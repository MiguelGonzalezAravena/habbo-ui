Maximize Your Mixes [7/12/07]

DJ-Ducksauce

Tips to take your Trax from jingle to jam

Thanks to the release of Trax, every Habbo has gotten the opportunity to express their musical tastes and showcase their musical talent for everyone to enjoy. One thing I’ve noticed, however, is that many people have commented that the music doesn’t sound real, or sounds too much like a ringtone. Although the Trax editor is set up to be simple enough for everyone to use it, it must be used properly in order to make the most of it and make the music sound natural. I connect my computer to my stereo and I can honestly say that if a mix is arranged well, it has both the quality and clarity of real music. Here are some tips to take your Trax to the next level.

1. Read the Masterclass modules!:
If you open the editor and find that you have difficulty arranging the items in your sound bank, you should first check out the Trax Masterclasses. If you haven’t ever studied music theory or taken a music composition class, Masterclass modules will teach you the basics. Sometimes people make Trax that seem unorganized, random sounds because they lack beat structure. Although, sometimes it is appropriate to be random in your execution as an expression of your personal style, being too random can make your music lack any cohesiveness.

2. Experiment with various genres:
You prefer only one or two musical genres but if you only work with one genre in Trax, you’re limiting your creative potential. There are some Trax that are very recognizable, for example the riffs used in “Snotty Day” or “Moshy Metal,” however, if you mix in other Trax, those recognizable sounds become more unique and people will be less likely to listen to your music with a “heard it before” mentality. Buying more Trax gives you more to work with and your mixes will have more depth. Try mixing genres that you don’t think wouldn’t normally be combined; you might be surprised at the outcome.

3. Try to develop new mixing techniques:
When you listen to music the volume of each instrument is not always the same, particularly in the percussion parts. Sometimes you will hear a part of the music get gradually louder, this is called a crescendo. You can mimic the effect of a crescendo in your own music by doubling or even tripling the same sound and staggering them in the editor. For instance, let’s say you have 6 bars of percussion and you wanted to insert a crescendo. You would start off using one two bar percussion piece, then in the next space you would place the same piece twice with one on top of the other, then you would do the same thing with three pieces. By overlapping the same sound, the volume will be intensified, thus producing a simulated crescendo.

4. Learn how to bring it all home:
The last part of a piece, usually added to bring it to a close is called the coda. You can make your coda more dramatic and engaging by overlapping the same sounds just like you do with the crescendo. Overlapping sounds that feed off of each other can produce a coda that sounds more interesting than it would if I just ended it with a single four bar piece.

Try using some of these techniques and I guarantee you music will become much more dynamic and entertaining for all of your Trax party guests.
