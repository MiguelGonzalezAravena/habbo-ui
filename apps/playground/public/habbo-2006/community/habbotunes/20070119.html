Habbo Tunes

Artist: Depapepe
Album: Let’s Go!
Formed in 2002, they gather a circle of people wherever they perform on the street. With only two acoustic guitars, they articulate imagined sceneries and emotions; their heart-filling, swinging sound and yearning melody provide a comfortable, enjoyable and highly compassionate atmosphere. Buoyed by the rapidly expanding instrumental music market, one of them gives full play to his talent as melody maker, while the other sensitively yet boldly embodies his emotions with help of his guitar. I would recommend the song “Start” to listen to. It’s fast, lively and bold. Sit back relax, play Habbo Hotel and listen to the guitars of Depapepe.

Artist: Sammy Davis Jr.
Album: The Millennium Collection: Best Of Sammy Davis Jr.
Well what can I say, this album is one of the best I’ve ever listened to. Sammy Davis Jr. is an old singer and sadly passed away but his music is still loved by many! Sammy Davis Jr. sang many songs alone and also in the group titled the Rat Pack, along with Frank Sinatra and Dean Martin. I love lots of songs sung by the Rat Pack, but I especially like the songs “The Candyman” which is featured in the movie Willy Wonka and the Chocolate Factory and I also enjoy the song “Mr.Bojangles.” All the songs in the album are very good songs but those two songs are by far my favorite. Go out and buy this album at the nearest store, you won’t be disappointed.

Artist: Rage Against the Machine
Album: Renegades
Rage Against the Machine is a hard rock band with some funk mixed in. Although they are no longer together, their guitarist, Tom Morello, has won many awards for his expertise with the funky guitar lines and crazy effects he uses. This album, in particular, is full of covers of other bands. Two of my favorite songs on the album are “The Ghost of Tom Joad” (in which they covered the legendary Bruce Springsteen) and “Maggie’s Farm” (a cover of Bob Dylan). These two, and many other songs, put a totally different but great feel on the original. Other popular songs off of this album that you may have heard are “How I Could Just Kill a Man” and “Renegades of Funk” which are both created by Rage. I strongly recommend this album, as a big fan, because it shows the true genre they play and how swiftly they execute it. “We’re the Renegades of Funk.”

Artist: Reel Big Fish
Album: Greatest Hit... And More
This album is one of my favorites. This band and what they do is off the hook! They literally are one of the few ska (ska is usually rock instruments like guitar and drums but with a reggae sound and trumpets) bands that are making albums and selling! This album is of all of their top songs, or just the ones they like the most. Either way it’s a really good album, and the songs are awesome, too. Like their first song “Sell Out.” This song is catchy and a cool song. Plus, the message of the song is something some people can relate to! Another good song is “Cheer Up,” because obviously this song is trying to send a message that you’re not the only one with problems in the world. What the lyrics say is kinda harsh, but true, and it cheers me up, might cheer you up, too. I think this song is another catchy one, and it’s “Don’t Start A Band.” I think it’s the best because it shows that starting a band can end up bad. And finally, their best song in my opinion is “Beer,” for two reasons. I like the tune of the song, and because it’s one of their earliest songs. That’s why I think the song is so awesome! I hope Reel Big Fish will continue on rockin` and hopefully will keep ska punk up to be recognized! Great band, great album. Listen to it if you want, I recommend it.

Artist: Nas
Album: Illmatic
I wasn’t that much of a hip hop fan until I tuned into Nas’1994 album “Illmatic,” which is considered one of the most legendary albums of all time. The album has some amazing songs and Nas has truly proved to be the best of the best. The album itself was rated 4 and a half to 5 stars by different sources. Some hit tracks from “Illmatic” include: “The World Is Yours,” “Halftime,” and “It Ain’t Hard To Tell.” This album has changed my perspective on hip hop and it probably will change yours as well.
