Habbo Tunes

Artist: Hellogoodbye
Album: Zombies! Aliens! Vampires! Dinosaurs!
Hellogoodbye is one of my favorite bands. They’re original. They capture the reality of everyday problems and events in everyday life. Not only are their songs meaningful, their singing and the music just makes you want to dance or close your eyes and relax. Their music is great for parties or just dancing around in your room like a complete idiot. I listen to their music while working out. It makes me want to run around. My favorite songs are “Here in Your Arms,” “Homewrecker,” “Touch Down Turn Around,” and “Two Weeks In Hawaii”.

Artist: Pink Spiders
Album: Teenage Graffiti
This album is an astounding example of new age emo / punk rock. The Pink Spiders have numerous songs on this album that are amazing singles. It’s not offensive but still has great lyrics and messages in the songs. Most notably, unlike many emo or punk rock albums, all of their songs are different sounding and have unforgettable melodies. One of their songs, “Adalae” has a very classic sound with almost a hint of reggae in it. I’d advise this album to any fan of rock music so, I guess all Habbos like it! So check it out!

Artist: Above & Beyond
Album: Tri State
I don’t normally bother writing reviews because I find it hard to describe music with words (lush, deep, rich, hypnotic, etc.) but, as with albums from BT, I felt the need to put something in print here. Tri State is one of the best albums I’ve purchased in the last 5 years. I remember when I first played Tiesto’s “In Search of Sunrise” how diverse yet strangely compelling it sounded. You wanted to listen again and again and each time you found something new. This is the same. Right from the off - beautiful piano - each track brings its own qualities and the whole lot flows quite brilliantly. “Stealing Time,” “Liquid Love” and “Alone Tonight” weave strong vocals into the mix and are all worthy of a ‘number one’ but let’s hope they stay here - the last thing we need is a ‘radio edit’ demolishing them. Yes, it is good background music, it’s good music to play in the car doing 90 up the M1, but really, this should be LISTENED to, on a decent sound system, with no distractions. So, there we are - another pointless review that didn’t really tell you anything about the album but I hope that this review stands out and encourages you to give this a listen so you can see what we’re all on about!

Artist: “Weird” Al Yankovic
Album: Straight Outta Lynwood
Do you ever wonder if you can find any good comedy artist? Well “Weird” Al is the right artist for you. “Weird” Al’s music is mostly all comedy that is originated off of other artist music. Some of the great hits of “Weird” Al are “White and Nerdy” which is originated from the song “Ridin’.” Another great hit is “Trapped in the Drive-through” which is originated from R.Kelly’s Trapped in the Closet. But “Weird” Al also makes his own music such as “I’ll Sue Ya,” “Weasel Stomping Day,” and “Do I Creep You Out.” So if you are ever wondering if there is any good comedy artist think “Weird” Al.

Artist: Jay-Z
Album: The Blueprint
Jay-Z again serves up an all-you-can-eat buffet of his ego on “The Blueprint.” Listening to “The Blueprint” from start to finish will have you loosening your belt because you’ve had too much of it. What else is there to expect from one rap’s biggest stars? That aside, Jay-Z still manages to piece together an album that won’t disappoint any fans. Of course, he throws in a couple of jams that are guaranteed hits to keep on him on top of the rap game, such as “Girls, Girls, Girls” and “IZZO (H.O.V.A.).” But he sneaks a few surprises in also, like the moody “Song Cry” in which Jay-Z gets true over a sample from “Sounds Like a Love Song” by Bobby Glen.
