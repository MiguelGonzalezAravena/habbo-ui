Scammer Jammer of the Week [10/24/06]

Becs

SPOOFY Mail or SPOOKY Mail?

(begin lead in)
Dear Habbo, It has come to our attenshun that you're account has been stolen... (end lead in)


...and we have safety band it. Please send your password, full name, and date of birth so we can return your account to you safly. Sinceerly, Becs

Yah...right. You fall for that email up there and you can kiss your Habbo account goodbye.

What's your first clue that the email above is fake? Hopefully the spelling. I can spell (99% of the time, that is). Most mail spoofers can't spell if their life depends upon it. They'd come in like 400th in a school spelling bee.

How do you tell if an email that LOOKS like it's from a staff member is fake? Weeeelllly welly welly, we just happen to have the answer for you. We like to use full headers when we suspect an email as being <*}}}}>< fisheh. Here's how to check full headers:

NOTE: If you're in a hurry and hate reading safety articles, here's the quick way to find your email client and see exactly how to read full headers:

http://www.spamcop.com/help_with_headers/ (link, click me to learn how to find a full header)

If you want to be smart and learn right here, right now...keep reading, grasshopper.

Oki, here's what a full header looks like (example below):

Received: from mail.bieberdorf.edu (mail.bieberdorf.edu [124.211.3.78]) by mailhost.immense-isp.com (8.8.5/8.7.2) with ESMTP id LAA20869 for lt;tmh@immense-isp.com>; Tue, 18 Mar 1997 14:39:24 -0800 (PST)
Received: from alpha.bieberdorf.edu (alpha.bieberdorf.edu [124.211.3.11]) by mail.bieberdorf.edu (8.8.5) id 004A21; Tue, Mar 18 1997 14:36:17 -0800 (PST)
From: rth@bieberdorf.edu R.T. Hood)
To: tmh@immense-isp.com
Date: Tue, Mar 18 1997 14:36:14 PST
Message-Id: <rth031897143614-00000298@mail.bieberdorf.edu>
X-Mailer: Loris v2.32
Subject: Lunch today?

The example above shows from where the email originated, which ISP routed the email, the original email address of the sender. Internet or e-mail headers are best read from the bottom up. Read upwards until you get to the origin of the email. The most important information follows the “Return-path:” and the “Reply-to:” fields. If these are different, the email is not who it says it’s from.

Here's a picture of the description of an email header: http://www.lse.ac.uk/itservices/help/emailheader.htm (link, full e-mail header picture, click here). Come back after you look at that picture and finish reading.
